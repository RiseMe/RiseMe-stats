# Methodology

This document describes exactly how every figure in `data/` is produced. Each monthly folder is generated by a single script run against RiseMe's job database, and nothing is edited by hand.

## Source data

RiseMe ingests job postings directly from employer applicant tracking systems: Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters and Workable boards, polled every few hours. It also ingests aggregated ATS feeds and a small number of remote-job boards. Each posting keeps its source, its publish date, the date we first saw it, and the date it closed.

Every posting is enriched with a job family, industry, seniority level and people-management flag. Salary is taken **only** from the employer's structured pay fields. RiseMe's own salary estimates are never used in these files.

## Which postings are counted

A posting is counted in month *M* when **all** of these hold:

1. **Published in month *M*** (UTC), according to the source's publish date.
2. **New, not republished.** We first saw it no more than 7 days before *M* began. Some ATSs re-stamp the publish date when an employer reposts an old opening, and those reposts are excluded.
3. **A direct employer.** Postings by staffing agencies and recruitment firms are excluded, as are postings from job aggregators and reposting sites, using RiseMe's employer classification.
4. **In scope geographically.** Located in the United States or Canada, or remote with no stated country. Postings with a recognisable location elsewhere are excluded.
5. **Counted once.** Rows with the same employer, title, city, state/province and work arrangement count as one posting, so a job syndicated by two sources isn't double-counted. The same title in different cities still counts separately. Duplicate employer records are merged when they share a domain: a subdomain counts as the parent only when some posting in the month is on the parent domain itself (`corporate.ford.com` with `ford.com`), so a tenant of a hosting platform is not merged into other tenants.

`summary.json` → `exclusions` reports how many rows each rule removed.

## Definitions

**Role** (`roles.csv`): matched on the job title using the same definitions as RiseMe's public job pages, which are linked in `riseme_url`.
- Title roles such as "Data engineer" require the title phrase.
- Broad roles such as "Software", "Data" and "AI & machine learning" also include postings whose enriched job family matches.
- Roles overlap: a senior software engineer posting counts toward "Software", "Software engineer" and "Senior software engineer".
- Roles with fewer than 150 postings in the month are omitted, because a narrow title definition would understate the size of the occupation.

**Role family** (`role-families.csv`): the job family assigned during enrichment. Each posting has exactly one, so families don't overlap.

**Work arrangement:** remote, hybrid or on-site, as stated by the employer or inferred from the posting text. Shares use only postings where the arrangement is known.

**Metro** (`metros.csv`): non-remote postings (on-site, hybrid, or the 0.4% with no stated arrangement) located in the metro area, using RiseMe's metro definitions (core city plus suburbs, constrained to the right state or province). Remote postings are not assigned to a metro.

**Seniority:** six bands from the enriched level, or from the title when enrichment is missing: Entry, Mid, Senior, Lead / Manager, Principal / Director and VP / Executive. "Lead / Manager" includes frontline and store managers.

**AI & machine learning:** postings whose title names AI or ML work (for example "AI engineer", "machine learning", "generative AI", "computer vision", "applied AI"), or whose enriched family is AI/ML.

**Visa sponsorship language:** whether the posting **explicitly** says the employer will (`yes`) or will not (`no`) sponsor a work visa. Most postings say neither, so the two shares don't sum to 100%.

**People management:** the posting requires managing direct reports.

## Salary

- **Disclosure** (`salary_posted_pct`): the share of postings with a minimum or maximum pay value in the posting's structured data. A range written only in the description text isn't detected, so disclosure is a lower bound.
- **Annualisation:** hourly × 2,080, daily × 260, weekly × 52, monthly × 12. Postings with an unknown pay period are excluded from salary statistics but still count as disclosing.
- **Midpoint:** the midpoint of the range, or the single value when only one is given.
- **Excluded values:** ranges wider than 3× (for example "$50k–$250k" placeholders) and annualised values below $20,000 or above $1,000,000 are excluded from salary statistics.
- **Currency:** US figures are USD from US postings. Canadian metros and provinces are reported in CAD. Other currencies are excluded.
- **Rounding:** medians and quartiles are rounded to the nearest $1,000.

## Pay-range laws

`posting_pay_range_law` = `yes` for jurisdictions whose law required a pay range in the job posting itself for the whole report month. As of October 2026, this list is:

- **US:** California, Colorado, District of Columbia, Hawaii, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, New York, Vermont, Washington
- **Canada:** British Columbia, Ontario

Laws that only require disclosure on request or at offer stage (for example Connecticut) are marked `no`. City-level ordinances are not modelled.

`salary-transparency-by-region.csv` covers non-remote postings only, because a remote posting may be covered by the law of wherever the candidate lives. Regions with fewer than 300 such postings are omitted. If a law is missing or misdated, please open an issue.

## Trend panel

Month-over-month change is the hardest figure to get right, because coverage changes look exactly like hiring changes:

- **New sources inflate the latest month.** Totals across all sources rise whenever a new source or employer board starts being tracked. A board added mid-month also contributes its still-open older jobs, while the ones that already closed are lost.
- **Dead boards deflate it.** A board that goes quiet because the employer switched ATS looks like a hiring freeze.
- **Moving publish dates fake growth.** Republished jobs move into later months.

So trends (`trend-panel.csv`, every `panel_mom_pct` column and `summary.json` → `trend_panel`) use a fixed panel of employers:

- Their **Greenhouse or Lever** board was polled in real time in the first half of the window's first month, meaning at least one job was ingested within 3 days of being published.
- Their board was **still being polled** in the report month's final 4 days.
- Only Greenhouse and Lever postings are used, because both keep a stable original publish date.

Changes compare **new postings per weekday**, so months with different numbers of weekends compare fairly.

A change is withheld (blank) when:
- either month has fewer than 150 postings, or
- a single employer accounts for more than 20% of either month's postings, because one company's hiring wave is not a market trend.

`summary.json` → `trend_panel` also reports how many panel employers posted more or less than the month before, since a flat total can hide broad declines.

The panel leans toward technology companies, which are the main users of Greenhouse and Lever, so read it as a technology-sector indicator.

## Thresholds

| Statistic | Withheld below |
|---|---|
| Share (any `_pct`) | 100 postings in the group |
| Salary median / quartiles | 50 postings with an annualised salary |
| Region in salary-transparency table | 300 on-site/hybrid postings |
| Role in `roles.csv` | 150 postings |
| Month-over-month change | 150 panel postings in each month, or any one employer above 20% |

Blank cells mean "withheld", not zero.

## Limitations

- **Not a census.** Employers that hire mainly through channels RiseMe doesn't track (some government, hourly and union hiring) are under-represented, so counts are comparable over time but not equal to total US or Canadian openings.
- **Enrichment errors.** Job families, seniority and work arrangement are model-assigned, and individual postings are occasionally misclassified. Aggregates over hundreds of postings are robust to this.
- **Publish dates come from the source.** For aggregated feeds, the publish date is the date the feed reports.

## Column reference

### `roles.csv`
| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `role` | Role name |
| `postings` | New postings in the month |
| `remote_pct`, `hybrid_pct`, `onsite_pct` | Work arrangement shares (of postings with a known arrangement) |
| `us_salary_posted_pct` | Share of US postings disclosing pay |
| `us_salary_n` | US postings with a usable annualised salary |
| `us_salary_p25_usd`, `us_salary_median_usd`, `us_salary_p75_usd` | Quartiles of posted US salary midpoints |
| `us_salary_150k_plus_pct` | Share of those salaries at or above $150,000 |
| `top_metros` | Three metros with the most on-site/hybrid postings for the role |
| `panel_mom_pct` | Month-over-month change in panel postings per weekday |
| `riseme_url` | Live postings for the role |

### `role-families.csv`
`role_family`, `postings`, `share_of_all_pct`, work-arrangement shares, seniority mix (`entry_pct` … `vp_exec_pct`), `people_management_pct`, `us_salary_posted_pct`, `us_salary_median_usd`, `us_visa_sponsorship_stated_pct`, `us_no_sponsorship_stated_pct` and `panel_mom_pct`.

### `metros.csv`
`metro`, `onsite_or_hybrid_postings`, `hybrid_pct`, `salary_posted_pct`, `salary_currency` (USD or CAD), `salary_n`, `salary_median`, `salary_p25`, `salary_p75`, `top_role_families` and `riseme_url`.

### `salary-transparency-by-region.csv`
`country`, `region_code`, `region`, `posting_pay_range_law`, `onsite_or_hybrid_postings`, `salary_posted_pct`, `salary_median` and `salary_currency`.

### `seniority.csv`
`seniority`, `postings`, `share_pct`, `remote_pct` and US salary quartiles.

### `top-hiring-companies.csv`
`rank`, `company`, `postings`, `top_role_family`, `remote_pct` and `salary_posted_pct`.

### `trend-panel.csv`
`month`, `panel_companies`, `new_postings`, `weekdays`, `postings_per_weekday`, `ai_ml_postings`, `ai_ml_share_pct`, `remote_pct` and `salary_posted_pct`.
